Air breaks, travel gas, and VVAL-79.

A break is planned when you are breathing oxygen at the last stop depth or
shallower, or when CNS reaches the warning threshold. Config sets how long you
go before a break, how long it lasts, and which gas to take. Left blank, the
planner picks the leanest mix you carry that is still breathable at that depth,
which keeps a hypoxic back gas out of a 3 m break. Two treatments: Navy, where
the break is dead time and the stop simply grows, and Subsurface, where the
break is modelled on the break gas. The oxygen clock runs across stop changes
and excludes travel, so "break after 30" means thirty minutes of oxygen
wherever you breathed it.

Travel gas. A descent on a hypoxic back gas now starts on the leanest mix you
carry that is breathable at the surface and changes over at the first safe stop
depth. It costs no decompression.

VVAL-18 is now VVAL-79, and plans air and nitrox only. If you have planned
trimix on VVAL-18, replan it on VPM-B or on ZHL16-C with gradient factors. The
model now carries the published nine-compartment parameter set and reproduces
the U.S. Navy Diving Manual Rev. 7 no-decompression table to within a minute at
15 of 21 depths, always on the permissive side. A dive carrying helium is
refused with an
explanation rather than computed against numbers the Navy has never published.

Two defects found while auditing. A model name typed with capitals, such as
VVAL18, used to select Buhlmann silently; names are now read
case-insensitively and an unrecognised one says so on the plan. A stop that
cleared in exactly zero time printed "-0 minutes".

Config is now a plain list of controls. Every setting is explained in the
manual under Info, where it can be read end to end.
